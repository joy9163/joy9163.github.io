About me

Atmospheric chemistry · urban air quality · BVOCs · observation-driven ML

I am an undergraduate researcher in atmospheric science in the Sino-Finnish dual-degree program at Nanjing University and the University of Helsinki (Nanjing–Helsinki Institute in Atmospheric and Earth System Sciences). I am currently a visiting research intern at Stanford University, working with Prof. Yuan Wang on observation-constrained reconstruction of high-resolution urban isoprene emissions.

My research focuses on atmospheric chemistry, urban air quality, biogenic volatile organic compounds (BVOCs), and observation-driven machine learning. Recent work includes urban organic-aerosol regime analysis in Shanghai, boreal-forest new particle formation from multi-site particle number size distributions, and hydrological controls on tree-mortality risk in southeastern U.S. forests.

I plan to pursue graduate study at the intersection of atmospheric science and computational methods, with the goal of developing approaches that are both physically grounded and practically useful for air quality and climate problems.

Research interests

  • Atmospheric chemistry — urban air quality, BVOCs, new particle formation, particle number size distributions, organic aerosol dynamics
  • Climate extremes — hourly extreme precipitation, anthropogenic attribution, and regional climate over the Yangtze River Basin
  • Environmental data science — observation-driven machine learning, clustering and regime detection, generalized additive models, satellite and in situ data analysis
