About me

Atmospheric chemistry · climate extremes · environmental data science

I am an undergraduate student in Atmospheric Science at Nanjing University, currently on exchange at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology. My work sits at the intersection of atmospheric chemistry, climate extremes, and data-driven environmental research.

I am particularly interested in combining physical understanding with interpretable machine learning to study complex Earth system problems — from extreme precipitation attribution with CMIP6 models, to global particle number size distributions across 16 long-term stations, to organic aerosol regime transitions in urban Shanghai. Research experience across China, Finland, Hong Kong, and the United States has shaped how I approach these questions.

I plan to pursue graduate study at the intersection of atmospheric science and computational methods, with the goal of developing approaches that are both physically grounded and practically useful for understanding climate change impacts.

Research interests

  • Atmospheric chemistry — new particle formation, particle number size distributions, organic aerosol dynamics, urban air quality
  • Climate extremes — hourly extreme precipitation, anthropogenic attribution, regional climate over the Yangtze River Basin and the Tibetan Plateau
  • Environmental data science — interpretable machine learning, clustering and regime detection, generalized additive models, threshold and early-warning analysis
